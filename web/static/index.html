One loop, four steps

Each planting season feeds the next recommendation.

  1. Candidate cells are ranked by predicted cooling, times vulnerable residents, divided by cost. Some are deliberately left unplanted so they can serve as controls later.

      candidate cells ranked across four cities

  2. The city logs what it actually did: where, when, what kind and what it cost. Existing records come from each city's public tree inventory.

      dated plantings on record

  3. Each planted cell is compared with matched unplanted cells, before and after, across every clear summer Landsat scene. If an effect is too small to see, the tool says so.

      planted cells measured against controls

  4. Measured results retrain the prediction model. Every prediction is logged and scored once its outcome is measured, so the next ranking is checked against the last.

      predictions logged for scoring

The evidence so far, without the gloss

Street-tree plantings at these densities have not yet shown reliable cooling in satellite data. Most single cells are inconclusive: the change is smaller than a 30 m sensor can pick out. The tool reports that rather than tuning until an effect appears.

  • These are satellite surface temperatures taken late morning. They are not the air temperature people feel.
  • All four cities are temperate. Nothing here covers a hot, dry city.
  • "Detected" is a statistical label, not proof that planting caused the change. It includes warming.
